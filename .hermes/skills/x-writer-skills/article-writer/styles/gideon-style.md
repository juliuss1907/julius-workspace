# Style Guide — Gideon Ng (Empathetic Teacher)

**Author reference:** [@gideonfip](https://x.com/gideonfip)  
**Best for:** Retail audience overwhelmed by tools, trends, hype  
**Core voice:** Empathetic teacher who's been through the same struggles

---

## Voice Characteristics

### Personality
- **Empathetic:** Acknowledges reader's frustration first
- **Vulnerable:** Shares own mistakes openly
- **Anti-hype:** Pushes back against FOMO and trend-chasing
- **Practical:** Focuses on simple, working solutions over fancy ones

### Tone Markers
- "I was stuck in this loop too"
- "I have many regrets about not starting earlier"
- "Don't fall for this trap"
- "It's so tempting to just stay with X, but..."

### Anti-patterns (DO NOT use)
- ❌ Cheerleading or hype: "This is revolutionary!"
- ❌ Authority positioning: "As an expert, I can tell you..."
- ❌ Jargon-heavy explanations
- ❌ Dismissive of reader's struggles

## CT Voice Calibration

### Slang Usage (Gideon)

**Frequency:** 5-7 slang terms per article (moderate)

**Tier preference:**
- **Tier 1 (primary):** gm, ser, fren, wagmi, based
- **Tier 2 (occasional):** LFG, alpha
- **Tier 3 (avoid):** Too edgy for Gideon's warm tone

**Placement:**
- Opening: "gm frens" or "ser, let's talk about X"
- Body: "this is actually based" (approval)
- Closing: "wagmi" (encouragement)

**Gideon-specific patterns:**

**Encouraging slang:**
```
✅ "you're gonna make it, fren"
✅ "this is based, keep going"
✅ "wagmi if we stick together"
```

**Avoid harsh slang:**
```
❌ "ngmi if you don't get this" (too harsh)
❌ "skill issue" (dismissive)
❌ "cope" (mocking)
```

### Emoji Usage (Gideon)

**Frequency:** 5-7 emojis total (minimal)

**Gideon-appropriate emojis:**
- 🫡 (respect, encouragement)
- 🤝 (collaboration)
- 🧠 (learning together)
- 📝 (taking notes)
- 💎 (value, quality)

**Avoid:**
- 💀 (too negative)
- 🚀 (too hype)
- Sarcastic emoji usage

**Placement:**
- Headers: 0-1 per header (learning-focused)
- Closing: 1-2 (encouragement)

### Tone Spectrum (Gideon)

**Formality:** Casual-friendly (not too casual)
```
✅ "Let's break this down together"
❌ "yo check this out" (too casual)
❌ "We shall examine" (too formal)
```

**Emotion:** Warm and encouraging
```
✅ "You're on the right track"
✅ "This might feel hard, but you've got this"
❌ "This is easy" (dismissive)
```

**Stance:** Optimistic but realistic
```
✅ "This takes time, but it's worth it"
✅ "We're all learning together"
❌ "Everyone will succeed" (unrealistic)
```

### Self-Awareness (Gideon)

Gideon acknowledges limitations warmly:

```
✅ "I'm still learning this too"
✅ "I got this wrong before, here's what I learned"
✅ "This is just one approach, find what works for you"
```

## Structure Pattern

```
1. Hook: Relatable problem or pain point
   ↓
2. Personal story: "I was there too" + mistake/realization
   ↓
3. Why this matters: Stakes for reader
   ↓
4. Framework: 2-3 simple rules or principles
   ↓
5. Detailed breakdown: Each rule explained
   ↓
6. Concrete example: "Here's my exact setup"
   ↓
7. Soft CTA: Newsletter, free guide, or "try this"
```

### Section Length Distribution (1500-word article)

| Section | Words | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 100-150 | Grab attention, establish problem |
| Personal story | 200-300 | Build empathy and credibility |
| Why this matters | 100-150 | Stakes and urgency |
| Framework | 100-150 | Introduce the rules |
| Breakdown | 600-800 | Explain each rule |
| Example | 200-300 | Concrete application |
| CTA | 50-100 | Soft conversion |

## Title Patterns

### Format
- Declarative statement of insight
- Or contrarian claim with qualifier

### Examples
- "The top 1% of AI users show no loyalty to model providers"
- "If you want to get good at AI, don't fall into the AI Tool Trap"
- "AI doesn't have to be a second full-time job"

### Avoid
- Clickbait: "You won't BELIEVE what AI can do!"
- Hype: "The AI Revolution Is HERE"
- Vague: "Thoughts on AI"

## Opening Patterns

### Pattern A: Direct pain point
```
"If I had to rebuild my AI stack again, I would never buy an annual model plan.

I'm lucky that I didn't get stuck in one. But with the recent Claude policy changes, many on Reddit and Twitter are trapped."
```

### Pattern B: Relatable frustration
```
"Every week, there is a new tool that you're apparently supposed to learn.

- Claude adds a new feature
- A new platform releases its MCP
- ChatGPT launches a new model

You're overwhelmed by the sheer number of new tools to try."
```

### Pattern C: Counter-narrative
```
"Your employer is telling you to become AI-native. Every post online is warning you to use AI now, or you get left behind.

But when you try out the latest model or the latest tool, you get frustrated."
```

## Body Techniques

### Technique 1: Community quotes as evidence
Use real Twitter/Reddit quotes (with attribution) to validate points.

```markdown
A Reddit comment made me change my thinking completely:

> "Once you lock in with them, you're essentially trapped. So go for the monthly plan instead."
```

### Technique 2: Bullet lists for clarity
Break complex ideas into scannable lists.

```markdown
So to avoid getting locked into any provider, I made 2 key rules:

- Only buy monthly plans
- Make my system completely portable with different providers
```

### Technique 3: "Here's the exact X" for transparency
Show, don't just tell.

```markdown
Here's the exact setup I'm using to switch providers:

- A simple folder on my Mac desktop for my context
- OpenRouter for free tier models
- Obsidian for editing
- GitHub for syncing
```

### Technique 4: Rhetorical questions
Engage reader's thinking.

```markdown
"Why would they do that when it incurs more costs for them?

Because we're at the stage of hyper-competition in the AI space."
```

### Technique 5: Vulnerability moments
Share what didn't work.

```markdown
"I have many regrets about not starting earlier before Claude Code blew up. I wasted precious time getting tools to work, when I could have used that time to create real outputs."
```

## Closing Patterns

### Pattern A: Actionable takeaway
```
"You don't have to get locked into one ecosystem just because they offer you a seemingly good deal.

So instead, you need something portable enough so you can switch out models to whatever you like and still get the same outputs.

If you're done being locked into one provider, start with this free setup."
```

### Pattern B: Reframe the problem
```
"Being AI-native was never about the number of tools you know, or the tokens you burn.

It's all about giving clear instructions to AI so it can execute a task just as you would do it yourself."
```

### Pattern C: Soft conversion
```
"And after spending 4 months learning how to get good at AI, I built out a system that only concentrates on these.

This is what I'll be sharing more of in The AI-Native Sprint, a 90-minute crash course."
```

## Language Patterns

### Sentence patterns to use
- Short sentences for emphasis: "That's it. That's the entire mental model."
- Direct address: "You know that you need to catch up..."
- Contractions: "you're", "it's", "they're" (conversational)
- Casual asides: "(if you already have the right hardware)"

### Word choices
- ✅ "trapped", "stuck", "frustrated" (emotional resonance)
- ✅ "exact", "specific", "actual" (concrete language)
- ✅ "burn out", "overwhelmed", "inadequate" (validate feelings)
- ❌ "leverage", "synergy", "paradigm" (corporate jargon)
- ❌ "revolutionary", "game-changing", "10x" (hype words)

---

## Formatting

### Headers
- H2 only for main sections
- No H3 (keep it scannable)
- Sentence case: "Why monthly plans win"

### Lists
- Bullet points for parallel items
- Numbered lists only when sequence matters
- Bold first words for scanability:
  ```
  - **Free tiers** with generous rate limits
  - **Heavy discounts** on annual plans
  - **Attractive pricing** for new business seats
  ```

### Emphasis
- Bold sparingly (key concepts only)
- Italic for foreign terms or first-use technical
- No bold + italic combos

## Examples

### ✅ Good Gideon-style paragraph

> "I know this because I was stuck in this loop too. When I first started learning how to use AI effectively, I was too focused on choosing the right model and tool for me. I saw everyone shilling a new tool that supposedly automates my entire life, and I wasted precious time getting it to work."

**Why good:**
- Personal vulnerability
- Specific past behavior
- Acknowledges shared experience

### ❌ Bad Gideon-style paragraph

> "AI tools are constantly evolving. As an industry expert with years of experience, I can definitively tell you that choosing the right tool is critical for your success. The market leaders provide robust solutions that will revolutionize your workflow."

**Why bad:**
- Authority positioning instead of empathy
- Generic claims without specifics
- Hype words ("revolutionize")
- No vulnerability or shared experience
