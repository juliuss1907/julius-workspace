# Style Guide — Stacy Muur (Analytical Researcher)

**Author reference:** [@stacy_muur](https://x.com/stacy_muur)  
**Best for:** Institutional + sophisticated retail audience  
**Core voice:** Analytical journalist with institutional-grade rigor

---

## Voice Characteristics

### Personality
- **Neutral observer:** Reports findings, doesn't cheerlead
- **Data-driven:** Numbers and tables anchor every claim
- **Contrarian:** Challenges consensus with evidence
- **Professional:** Avoids emotional language

### Tone Markers
- "I've been watching X since the start of this year"
- "Most analysts are reading it wrong"
- "The data on X says otherwise"
- "Here's what most people miss"
- "I think the leaderboard is misleading"

### Anti-patterns (DO NOT use)
- ❌ Emotional appeals: "This is so exciting!"
- ❌ First-person vulnerability: "I was stuck too"
- ❌ Hype language: "groundbreaking", "revolutionary"
- ❌ Personal anecdotes as primary evidence

## CT Voice Calibration

### Slang Usage (Stacy)

**Frequency:** 3-5 slang terms per article (minimal)

**Tier preference:**
- **Tier 1 (selective):** ser, based, alpha
- **Tier 2 (rare):** Only when data-relevant
- **Tier 3 (avoid):** Too casual for analytical tone

**Placement:**
- Opening: "ser, the data shows X" (establish CT voice)
- Body: "this is based on [data]" (approval with evidence)
- Closing: Minimal slang (data speaks)

**Stacy-specific patterns:**

**Data-driven slang:**
```
✅ "the data is based"
✅ "alpha: 80% of projects fail within 6 months"
✅ "ser, the numbers don't lie"
```

**Avoid casual slang:**
```
❌ "wagmi frens" (too casual)
❌ "LFG" (too hype)
❌ "degen mode" (not analytical)
```

### Emoji Usage (Stacy)

**Frequency:** 3-5 emojis total (very minimal)

**Stacy-appropriate emojis:**
- 🔬 (research, analysis)
- 📊 (data, charts)
- 🎯 (precision, accuracy)
- 🧠 (insights)

**Avoid:**
- 🫡, 🤝 (too community-focused)
- 🚀, 🔥 (too hype)
- 💀 (too casual)

**Placement:**
- Headers: 0-1 per header (research-focused only)
- Body: Rare (data emphasis only)
- Closing: 0-1 (if CTA)

### Tone Spectrum (Stacy)

**Formality:** Professional but CT-aware
```
✅ "The data indicates X"
✅ "Analysis shows Y"
❌ "We shall examine" (too formal)
❌ "yo check this data" (too casual)
```

**Emotion:** Neutral and objective
```
✅ "The evidence suggests X"
✅ "Based on the data, Y is likely"
❌ "This is amazing!" (too emotional)
```

**Stance:** Evidence-based skepticism
```
✅ "The data doesn't support this claim"
✅ "More research is needed before concluding X"
❌ "I believe X" (opinion without data)
```

### Self-Awareness (Stacy)

Stacy acknowledges data limitations:

```
✅ "Sample size is limited, so take this with caution"
✅ "This analysis is based on available data as of [date]"
✅ "Correlation doesn't imply causation here"
```

## Structure Pattern

```
1. Thesis: Clear contrarian claim
   ↓
2. Market context: Data + scale
   ↓
3. Players overview: Who's in the space
   ↓
4. Comparative analysis: Side-by-side breakdown
   ↓
5. Key dimensions: 4-6 axes of comparison
   ↓
6. Implications: What this means
   ↓
7. Conclusion: Restate thesis with nuance
```

### Section Length Distribution (1800-word article)

| Section | Words | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis | 100-150 | Hook with contrarian claim |
| Market context | 200-300 | Establish scale and stakes |
| Players overview | 200-300 | Map the landscape |
| Comparative analysis | 200-300 | TL;DR comparison |
| Key dimensions | 700-900 | Detailed breakdown (largest section) |
| Implications | 200-300 | What matters, why |
| Conclusion | 100-150 | Restate thesis |

## Title Patterns

### Format
- Declarative claim with specific subject
- Often contrarian or counter-intuitive

### Examples
- "The tokenized stocks race is being won on the wrong scoreboard"
- "User's Guide to Boring Yields"
- "Most fintech teams don't have a user acquisition problem"

### Avoid
- Personal pronouns: "Why I think..."
- Questions: "Are tokenized stocks worth it?"
- Lists in title: "5 things about X"

## Opening Patterns

### Pattern A: Direct contrarian thesis
```
"The tokenized stocks race is being won on the wrong scoreboard.

I've been watching the tokenized stocks category since the start of this year. And I think most analysts are reading it wrong.

The market crossed $1 billion in distributed value this year, up from around $32 million twelve months ago."
```

### Pattern B: Pattern recognition
```
"Most fintech teams don't have a user acquisition problem.

They have a trust problem, an onboarding problem, or an activation problem.

And paid channels are usually just the first place where this becomes impossible to ignore."
```

### Pattern C: Reframe of common metric
```
"This space has made the 'slow and steady' approach not worth trying at times.

Even a 10x could feel underwhelming during periods of euphoria. But there's also a dark side to this."
```

## Body Techniques

### Technique 1: Comparative tables
Side-by-side comparison of platforms/products/strategies.

```markdown
| Platform | TVL | Volume | Architecture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ondo | $3.71B | $2.28B/mo | Spread pricing |
| xStocks | $381M | $20B+ cumulative | 1:1 with multipliers |
| Anchored | New | TBD | 1:1 direct |
```

### Technique 2: Data with context
Numbers always paired with what they mean.

```markdown
"APAC ETF AUM crossed $2 trillion in 2026 with 25% annual growth. China overtook Japan as the region's largest ETF market in 2025 and crossed $850 billion in AUM. The pace is the part worth sitting with: sixteen years to reach RMB 1 trillion in ETF assets, then four months to climb from RMB 4 to 5 trillion."
```

### Technique 3: Multi-dimensional analysis
Break comparison into specific axes.

```markdown
**Pricing simplicity.** A token that doesn't match the underlying ticker is a UX problem at retail scale.

**Real-time proof of reserves.** Quarterly attestations work until the first major custody failure. Then they don't.

**Fund products.** 100+ tokenized stocks live, almost no tokenized funds.
```

### Technique 4: Conditional logic
"If X holds, then Y becomes possible."

```markdown
"If the SEC grants Ondo's no-action letter, Ondo becomes the regulatory template for compliant tokenization in the US, which is a real moat."
```

### Technique 5: Risk tier framework
Classify items by clear criteria.

```markdown
**Ultra-low risk:** easy to withdraw, available to move at any moment
**Low risk:** the core stable yield for most users
**Medium risk:** yield exposure if you understand protocol risk
**Higher risk:** a short-term high ROI play
```

## Closing Patterns

### Pattern A: Implications for stakeholders
```
"If you're an allocator looking at this category over the next five years, the question isn't who's biggest today. It's whose architecture fits institutional adoption, fund-product expansion, and Asian capital coming onchain.

On those questions, the scoreboard looks very different from what's on rwa.xyz right now."
```

### Pattern B: Pattern summary
```
"The point of these tiers isn't to rank yields from best to worst.

It's to understand what kind of risk you're taking for each extra percentage point of return."
```

### Pattern C: Closing thesis restate
```
"The biggest TVL doesn't always win. In a category where regulation, custody, and architecture matter as much as distribution, the team that builds correctly outlasts the team that ships fastest."
```

## Language Patterns

### Sentence patterns to use
- Declarative statements: "Two platforms are running away with the headline metrics."
- Precise language: "ISAE 3000 audits, an international assurance standard"
- Comparative framing: "Platform A does X. Platform B does Y. Platform C does neither."
- Conditional reasoning: "If X, then Y. Otherwise, Z."

### Word choices
- ✅ "moat", "leverage", "alignment" (sophisticated finance)
- ✅ "infrastructure", "architecture", "structure" (systems thinking)
- ✅ "implies", "suggests", "indicates" (analytical hedging)
- ✅ Specific terms: "Type 9 license", "ISAE 3000", "ERC-20"
- ❌ "amazing", "incredible", "huge" (subjective intensifiers)
- ❌ "everyone", "always", "never" (overgeneralizations)

## Formatting

### Headers
- H2 for main sections
- H3 for sub-categories (especially in comparative analysis)
- Sentence case or descriptive: "Pricing is the most overlooked design choice"

### Lists
- Bullet points with bolded leading concept:
  ```
  - **Pricing simplicity.** A token that doesn't match the underlying ticker is a UX problem.
  - **Real-time proof of reserves.** Quarterly attestations work until the first major custody failure.
  ```

### Tables
Heavily used. Standard format:
```markdown
| Aspect | Platform A | Platform B | Platform C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metric 1 | Value | Value | Value |
```

### Emphasis
- Bold for entity names on first mention
- Italic for technical terms requiring definition
- No emoji except in TL;DR sections (sparingly)

## Examples

### ✅ Good Stacy-style paragraph

> "Two platforms are running away with the headline metrics. Ondo leads on TVL. xStocks leads on volume. A third platform, Anchored, just launched and is too small to compare on numbers, but the architectural decisions they've made point at a different audience entirely."

**Why good:**
- Specific entities named
- Clear positioning of each player
- Sets up comparison framework
- Hints at deeper analysis

### ❌ Bad Stacy-style paragraph

> "I've been so impressed with what's happening in the tokenized stocks space lately. There are so many cool projects and the growth has been absolutely incredible! I think we're going to see massive adoption in the coming years."

**Why bad:**
- Emotional language ("impressed", "cool", "incredible")
- Vague claims ("so many", "massive")
- No data or specifics
- Personal sentiment as evidence
