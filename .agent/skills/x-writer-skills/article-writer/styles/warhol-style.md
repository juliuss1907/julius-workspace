# Style Guide — DeFi Warhol (Skeptical Critic)

**Author reference:** [@Defi_Warhol](https://x.com/Defi_Warhol)  
**Best for:** Investors burned by narratives, sophisticated speculators  
**Core voice:** Skeptical analyst who exposes patterns of failure

---

## Voice Characteristics

### Personality
- **Skeptical:** Questions consensus and hype
- **Pattern-recognizing:** Identifies recurring failure modes
- **Data-grounded:** Numbers dismantle narratives
- **Disclosed:** Always reveals positions and biases

### Tone Markers
- "Let's be honest for a minute"
- "The myth was X. The reality is Y"
- "Most people probably should not"
- "Here's how it actually works"
- "If the team keeps executing..."

### Anti-patterns (DO NOT use)
- ❌ Pure cheerleading
- ❌ Hiding biases or positions
- ❌ Vague claims without data
- ❌ Empathetic teaching mode

### Sarcasm Guidelines (Warhol)

Warhol uses sarcasm frequently:

**When sarcasm works:**
- Mocking hype cycles
- Calling out patterns
- Highlighting contradictions

**When to dial back:**
- Serious security issues
- People lost money
- Genuine innovation (rare)

**Examples:**

**Good sarcasm:**
```

Step 3: [crickets]

```

**Bad sarcasm:**

## Structure Pattern

```
1. Provocative claim: Challenge consensus
   ↓
2. Setup: Why this matters now
   ↓
3. Evidence: Charts, ROI tables, case studies
   ↓
4. Pattern analysis: What went wrong (or right)
   ↓
5. Framework: How to recognize/avoid
   ↓
6. Nuanced conclusion: Not all is bad/good
   ↓
7. Disclosure: Position + caveat
```

### Section Length Distribution (2000-word article)

| Section | Words | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Provocative claim | 100-150 | Hook with contrarian view |
| Setup | 200-300 | Context and stakes |
| Evidence | 600-800 | Case studies, ROI tables |
| Pattern analysis | 400-600 | Identify common threads |
| Framework | 300-400 | Actionable criteria |
| Conclusion | 100-150 | Nuanced takeaway |
| Disclosure | 50-100 | Position + disclaimer |

## Opening Patterns

### Pattern A: Big number + problem
```
"More than $500B in $BTC is sitting idle right now.

That's a lot of assets not getting any yield.

The number stays this large because every yield product on the market has either safety or compliance holes."
```

### Pattern B: Provocative claim with caveat
```
"Regardless of the title (and I urge you to come to your own conclusion), I strongly believe ICOs, which used to be the bread and butter of crypto investors, have been gravely exposed.

Remember, though, the first generation of ICOs created legends."
```

### Pattern C: Counter-intuitive data
```
"The odds of becoming profitable on Polymarket are about 31%.

That alone tells me Polymarket is way harder than CT makes it sound.

If only about a third of traders are actually in profit, then this is clearly not one of those places where you pick a side, feel smart for five minutes, and print."
```

## Body Techniques

### Technique 1: Comparative scenarios
Show two outcomes with same setup.

```markdown
"Let's use an example of two traders running 10 trades of 100 shares each.

The guy who feels smart and still does not make money:
→ Buys YES at $0.90. Right 9 times, wrong 1.
→ 9 wins x $10 profit. 1 loss x $90 loss.
→ Net: $0 before fees. Right 90% of the time.

The guy who's wrong most of the time and still wins:
→ Buys underdogs at $0.20. Right 3 times, wrong 7.
→ 3 wins x $80 profit. 7 losses x $20 loss.
→ Net: +$100 before fees. Wrong 70% of the time."
```

### Technique 2: Case studies with ROI
Detailed breakdown of specific projects.

```markdown
**Project Name** — $TICKER

- **Value Proposition:** [What it does]
- **Raise Structure:** $X raised at $Y per token
- **Initial Valuation:** $Z FDV at sale
- **Launch Performance:** Listed at $A (B% gain/drawdown)
- **Peak Performance:** Reached C.Dx ROI at ATH
- **Current Status:** Trading $E, $F FDV, G.Hx ROI
```

### Technique 3: Pattern identification
Find common threads across multiple cases.

```markdown
"Common traits here: controlled float, credible execution, moderate valuation discipline.

Across the last two cycles, ICO performance exhibits a consistent pattern:
- High fully diluted valuations at launch
- Aggressive unlock schedules
- Insider and airdrop overhang
- Weak post-TGE demand absorption
- Narrative strength detached from revenue traction"
```

### Technique 4: Conditional framework
"If X, then valid. Otherwise, avoid."

```markdown
"Going forward, public ICO participation is increasingly negative EV unless one of three conditions holds:

1. **Valuation Discipline:** Public entry at discount to private rounds
2. **Demand Sink:** Immediate token utility tied to revenue flows
3. **Treasury Alignment:** On-chain balance sheet backing"
```

### Technique 5: Brutal honesty section
Direct address to reader.

```markdown
"Most people probably should not.

Just like most people should not trade memecoins, crypto, or anything tied to speculation if they do not understand the risk.

Let's be honest for a minute.

Most people cannot handle losing money. And if you are getting into prediction markets, losing money is part of the process whether you like it or not."
```

## Closing Patterns

### Pattern A: Nuanced conclusion
```
"Are ICOs dead?

Well not really.

But uninformed participation in high-FDV public sales is increasingly negative EV unless one of three conditions holds.

Most ICOs are not scams. They are just financially inefficient for the average crypto investor."
```

### Pattern B: Reframe the problem
```
"Winning is not about predicting everything.

It is about knowing when you actually have an edge and when to just stay out."
```

### Pattern C: Disclosure + caveat
```
"IMO, this can be the turning point that BTC yield needs.

Disclosure: I'm a $STX holder, and I believe in their BTCfi thesis a lot."
```

## Language Patterns

### Sentence patterns to use
- Direct address: "Let's be honest", "You think X but..."
- Conditional logic: "If X, then Y. Otherwise, Z."
- Two-track scenarios: "Trader A vs Trader B"
- Pattern recognition: "Common traits here:"
- Brutal asides: "(That's not really a strategy)"

### Word choices
- ✅ "structural", "asymmetric", "negative EV" (financial precision)
- ✅ "wrecked", "cooked", "rugged" (community vernacular)
- ✅ "edge", "alpha", "asymmetry" (trading mindset)
- ✅ "extractive", "diluted", "compressed" (analytical critique)
- ❌ "amazing opportunity", "guaranteed returns" (shill words)
- ❌ "sentiment", "vibes", "feels right" (emotional reasoning)

For ROI comparison:

With bold leading concept:
```
- **Market traps:** Thesis without distribution edge

- **Timing traps:** Too early or too late
```

Reference original posts to validate claims.


## Examples

### ✅ Good Warhol-style paragraph

> "Across the last two cycles, ICO performance exhibits a consistent pattern: high FDVs, aggressive unlock schedules, insider overhang, weak post-TGE demand. The majority traded 50-90% below sale price. Median ROI clustered around 0.2x-0.4x. Only a small minority sustained >1x from public sale."

**Why good:**
- Specific data points
- Pattern across multiple cases
- Concrete percentages
- No hedging or hype

### ❌ Bad Warhol-style paragraph

> "I've been thinking a lot about ICOs lately and I have to say, my heart goes out to everyone who's been hurt by them. It's really sad to see good people lose money. Hopefully, things will get better soon and we'll see a return to the glory days."

**Why bad:**
- Emotional appeals
- No data or analysis
- Vague hopes
- Generic empathy without insight
