# Type Guide — Hot Take (Opinion)

**Purpose:** Share contrarian or strong opinion on current topic  
**Best for:** React to news, challenge consensus, thought leadership

---

## Structure Pattern

```
[Hook: Contrarian claim or provocative statement]

[Reasoning: Why you believe this]

[Implication or example]

[Optional: CTA or question]
```

---

## Hook Patterns

### Pattern A: "Hot take:" prefix
```
"🔥 Hot take: crypto's biggest competitor isn't traditional banks."
"Hot take: the biggest security threat to crypto isn't hacks..."
```

### Pattern B: Greeting + claim
```
"Morning, let's talk crypto over breakfast ☕🍳

Hot take: [claim]"
```

### Pattern C: Direct contrarian statement
```
"Everyone was expecting 'bullish' after the Clarity Act passed 👇

Here's what actually happened:"
```

---

## Body Development

### 3-part structure:

**1. Claim** (what you believe)
```
"crypto's biggest competitor isn't traditional banks.

It's inconvenience."
```

**2. Reasoning** (why you believe it)
```
"Most people don't reject crypto because they hate blockchain.
They reject it because the experience is exhausting.

Too many wallets.
Too many bridges.
Too many fees."
```

**3. Implication** (what this means)
```
"That's why projects focused on simplification are underrated.

@ProjectName feels less like a 'crypto project' and more like someone asked: 'What if digital money just worked?'"
```

---

## Emoji Usage

**Placement:**
- Hook emphasis: 🔥, 🚨, ⚛️
- Reasoning breaks: 👇, ☕, 🍳
- Implication: 💸, 🔔, 💭

**Pattern:**
- 2-3 emojis total
- Front-loaded (hook + first line)
- Avoid in middle of reasoning

---

## CTA Patterns

**Pattern A: Implicit (no explicit CTA)**
```
[Ends with implication, lets it sit]
```

**Pattern B: Project tag**
```
"@ProjectName"
```

**Pattern C: Soft question**
```
"Thoughts?"
"Am I wrong?"
```

---

## Tone Calibration

### Confident but not arrogant
✅ "Hot take: X isn't Y. It's Z."
❌ "If you don't see this, you're ngmi."

### Contrarian but not dismissive
✅ "Most people think X, but actually Y."
❌ "Everyone is wrong about X."

### Provocative but not inflammatory
✅ "The biggest threat isn't what you think."
❌ "You're all idiots for believing X."

## Examples

### ✅ Good Hot Take

**Example 1: Inconvenience angle**
```
Morning, let's talk crypto over breakfast ☕🍳

Hot take:
crypto's biggest competitor isn't traditional banks.

It's inconvenience.

Most people don't reject crypto because they hate blockchain.
They reject it because the experience is exhausting.

Too many wallets.
Too many bridges.
Too many fees.
Too many steps.

That's why projects focused on simplification are underrated.

@KoloHub feels less like a "crypto project" and more like someone asked:
"What if digital money just worked?"

No dramatic marketing.
No "financial revolution" speeches.
Just smoother payments, easier access, and fewer headaches.

Ironically, that simplicity might be what finally pushes crypto mainstream.
```
**Why good:** Clear claim, specific reasoning, real implication

**Example 2: Security angle**
```
🔥 Hot take: the biggest security threat to crypto isn't hacks, rugs, or exploits.

It's a quantum computer in a datacenter somewhere ⚛️ quietly making your private key public knowledge.

🚨 No alarm.
🔔 No alert.
💸 Just gone.

@quipnetwork
```
**Char count:** 254 / 280  
**Why good:** Dramatic setup, specific threat, visceral imagery

### ❌ Bad Hot Take

**Example: Vague claim**
```
Hot take: crypto is going to change everything soon.

The future is bright and everyone will use it.

Bullish! 🚀
```
**Why bad:** No contrarian angle, vague claim, no reasoning

---

## Type-Specific Tips

1. **Lead with the claim:** Don't bury it in paragraph 3
2. **Be specific:** "Inconvenience" > "Problems"
3. **Use concrete examples:** "Too many wallets" > "It's complicated"
4. **Avoid hedging:** "I think maybe" weakens the take
5. **End strong:** Implication or project mention, not generic hype
